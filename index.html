02 How I work

01

Make the bug happen before I fix it

Most of the bugs I get cannot be made to happen on command, which is what makes them hard. For the duplicate inventory bug I slowed my browser to 3G speed and kept switching tabs while saving, for days, until it finally happened. If you cannot make a bug appear, you cannot prove you fixed it.

02

Logs before theories

Before I settle on what is causing a problem, I look at what the system actually recorded. I read the logs in Datadog and add my own logging to the live code. That is how the duplicate products turned out to be merchants pressing Save during the 30 seconds when the app was syncing in the background. Nothing in the bug report pointed there.

03

Measure the real thing

I tested the storefront's speed on a real production build, not on the development server (ng serve) running on my laptop. The development server skips the caching that makes a live site fast, so the score it gives you is not the score your users get. Tuning against that number wastes weeks.

04

Say when the fix belongs elsewhere

Once the storefront reached 85, the rest of the delay was coming from how the backend fetched products, not from anything on the frontend. I said so and handed it to the right person, rather than spend another week chasing points I could not win.

01 Raised the storefront Lighthouse score from 60 to 85 +25 pts

Problem. Every merchant's public store loaded slowly and the score wouldn't move past the low 60s.

What I did. Migrated the whole Angular app to lazy-loaded standalone routes, then code-split a 643 KB checkout chunk by deferring the Google Maps loader and the components behind it. Consolidated font loading into one request, moved images to WebP at their real display size, set fetchpriority="high" on the LCP banner, and added long-term cache headers. I tested with production builds instead of ng serve, since ng serve doesn't apply real cache headers and the scores from it are misleading.

Result. 60 to 85. Past that point the remaining bottleneck was product fetching on the backend, not the frontend, so I said so instead of spending more time on the wrong layer.

02 Fixed a race condition that duplicated merchants' product variants data integrity

Problem. Merchants were ending up with duplicate product variants. It didn't happen every time and I couldn't reproduce it at first.

What I did. I added debugging to the add-product flow and found the window. If a merchant hit Save at the same moment the 30-second background upload sync ran, both paths sent the same unsent events, and there was no lock stopping that. I added an is_sending flag across the sync services and switched the variant writes to a single bulkAdd.

Result. No more duplicates, and product creation writes faster.

03 Stopped product edits from reverting after save merchant trust

Problem. Merchants reported that their edits went back to the old values, and that new products disappeared from the inventory list for a while.

What I did. There are two background workers that don't know about each other. Download sync runs every 60 seconds, upload sync every 30. If download ran first it overwrote the local changes with the older server data. I made the download path check for pending unsent events on each item and skip the overwrite if there are any.

Result. Local changes stay until they're actually pushed. Shipped to production.

Sync workersRace conditionsDatadog

04 Turned an N+1 query in checkout into one batched lookup N+1 → O(1)

Problem. Completing an order could hang for minutes. Merchants hit this on every sale.

What I did. I profiled the shipping options validation and found product variants being fetched once per line item. I batched them into one query and used a dictionary lookup.

Result. Database calls went from one per line item to one, which cut order completion time.

05 Made the signup to-do list load right away instead of 30 seconds late PH + TH

Problem. New merchants signed up and got an empty onboarding checklist, which is the first screen they see in the product.

What I did. The list was built by a synchronous loop that waited on the 30-second background sync worker. I parallelized it. That surfaced two more issues, so I fixed those too: completed steps were flashing in on slow connections, so the list now waits until the merchant data is actually synced before it builds, and the skeleton loader wasn't working, so I fixed that. Switching accounts now clears the old data as well.

Result. The checklist shows up right away. Shipped for both PH and TH.

AngularAsync orchestrationPerceived performance

06 Moved PH and TH production data into BigQuery data engineering

Problem. Reporting for both regions ran off queries against the production databases, and Philippine and Thailand data lived in separate places.

What I did. Set up the PH and TH data in BigQuery so the reporting and analytics side reads from a warehouse instead of hitting production directly, with both regions in one place.

Result. Analytics queries no longer compete with live merchant traffic, and PH and TH numbers can be looked at side by side.
